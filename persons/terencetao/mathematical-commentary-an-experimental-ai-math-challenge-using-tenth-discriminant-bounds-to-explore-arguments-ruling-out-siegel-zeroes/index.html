一项实验性 AI 数学挑战：用第十判别式的界探索排除西格尔零点的论证

Terence Tao 2026-10-20

In the spirit of my recent “ Math 2.0 ” talk on new ways to use AI assistance to enhance the human understanding of mathematics, rather than substitute for it, I am using this post to propose an experimental challenge, inspired by the recent activity around excluding Landau–Siegel zeroes, as discussed in this previous post.

There is a well-known connection between Landau–Siegel zeroes and the tenth discriminant problem, which I will briefly recall here; an in-depth discussion on this problem can be found in this survey of Shafarevich. As far back as the work of Gauss, it was observed that there were nine discriminants −d{-d}−d whose rings of integers for the associated quadratic field Q(−d){{\bf Q}(\sqrt{-d})}Q(−d​) enjoyed unique factorization, namely

d=1,2,3,7,11,19,43,67,163.\displaystyle d = 1, 2, 3, 7, 11, 19, 43, 67, 163.d=1,2,3,7,11,19,43,67,163.

Gauss believed this list of nine to be the complete list of such discriminants (the “class number one problem”), but this was only proven by Heegner in 1952 by introducing the concept of a Heegner point on the modular curve; and the above numbers are now known as Heegner numbers.

Prior to Heegner’s solution was the work of Heilbronn and Linfoot in 1934, who used what we now call the Deuring–Heilbronn repulsion phenomenon to show that the existence of a tenth discriminant in the above list can be used to “repel” any further such discriminants, so that there was at most one further Heegner number beyond the nine already known. However, this argument was ineffective: no upper bound on this putative tenth discriminant was given, and so there was no obvious way to solve the class number one problem purely from this result.

There is a close connection to Landau–Siegel zeroes: if d{d}d is a Heegner number, and χ{\chi}χ is the quadratic character associated to this quadratic field, then the class number formula gives L(1,χ)=πd{L(1,\chi) = \frac{\pi}{\sqrt{d}}}L(1,χ)=d​π​, and with a bit more work one can produce a Landau–Siegel zero L(β,χ)=0{L(\beta,\chi)=0}L(β,χ)=0 with 1−β≍1d{1-\beta \asymp \frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}}1−β≍d​1​. For sufficiently large d{d}d, this is inconsistent with Siegel’s theorem; but the standard proofs of this theorem also relied on the repulsion phenomenon and were also ineffective. It was only in 1976 that Goldfeld gave an effective version of Siegel’s theorem that was enough to at least bound the tenth discriminant, given a suitable high rank elliptic curve. In followup work of Oesterlé, combined with a high rank elliptic curve of Gross and Zagier, the bound d≤e7000{d \leq e^{7000}}d≤e7000 was obtained; using a later elliptic curve of Brumer and Kramer, proven to have high rank by Mestre, Oesterlé, and Serre, the bound was improved to d≤e55{d \leq e^{55}}d≤e55. See this survey of Goldfeld for more discussion.

But now we have a fair number of new (and effective) ways to rule out Siegel zeroes, either by establishing a quasi-Riemann hypothesis, or by some version of the argument in this previous post. In principle, each version of these arguments should also give a bound on the tenth discriminant d{d}d (without using Heegner’s solution to the class number one problem that rules out this discriminant). The task of making each of these estimates explicit enough to create such a bound is rather tedious for humans. However, it seems eminently suitable for AI.

Thus, the challenge I pose is, for each of the new manuscripts or digestions relating to either the quasi-Riemann hypothesis or the Landau–Siegel zero problem (or by some novel argument), to use an AI tool to work out an effective upper bound for a hypothetical tenth discriminant d{d}d, without using Heegner’s methods, and to make best efforts to optimize this constant. This serves several purposes:

  • If the threshold is low enough to resolve the remaining discriminants by direct computation, this would yield a new solution of the class number one problem — possibly one that is formalizable in Lean in a high quality fashion (suitable for instance for upstreaming to Mathlib).
  • By performing this exercise on each of the different writeups, we obtain a way to compare the “strength” of each such writeup (after performing whatever optimizations are available for each writeup), which could give insight as to the relative advantages or disadvantages of different arrangements of the methods.
  • The exercise of minimizing the upper bound may spur the creation of entirely new arguments to solve either the class number one problem, or the elimination of Landau–Siegel zeroes.
  • There are many previous “failed” attempts at excluding the Landau–Siegel zero. It is possible that some of them contain ideas that can be combined with the newer solutions to this problem to “repair” the proofs, or at least improve the bound on the tenth discriminant.
  • Efforts here may also help improve the state of knowledge on higher class number problems, which by the work of Watkins in 2004 is currently solved up to class number 100.

Furthermore, as both of these problems are already solved (for over seven decades, in the case of the class number one problem), there is little risk of such a competition inadvertently disrupting an ongoing research project.

I am therefore inviting interested readers to contribute in the comments any effective bounds on the tenth discriminant they can extract from either an existing argument, or a novel one, with any amount of AI use permitted for this challenge so long as it is disclosed. Note that the challenge is broader than simply getting the “best” bound overall amongst all possible approaches; I am also interested in what bounds one can extract from any specific approach, even if they are worse than, say, the bounds of Goldfeld or Oesterlé. In particular, “negative” results in which the bounds obtained from a given method end up being rather large are still scientifically useful pieces of information that are worth reporting. One could also explore other directions, such as bounds that avoid using extensive compute to squeeze out the maximal amount of optimization, but are instead clean enough that their arguments are human-verifiable.

Any large outputs should be posted as links rather than as plain text; for instance, pointing to a Github repository containing one’s work would be a reasonable way to submit an entry (but a human-generated summary should be provided in the main text of the comment).

I would ask contributors to not prioritize speed above quality; this is not intended to be a race regarding who is “first” to claim any given bound. The spirit is intended to be collaborative rather than competitive: think of a Polymath style project, updated for the AI era. In particular, as with traditional mathematical research, it would be appropriate to build upon previous contributions to obtain an improved bound for a given proof technique. (But in such cases, one should of course give appropriate attribution to the prior source.)

I will not insist on formal verification (e.g., in Lean) of the results, though I would ask anyone submitting a purely AI-generated solution to do enough due diligence to check the output (e.g., with an independent AI tool) before posting it here.

I am looking forward to the results of this experimental challenge, both for the mathematical insights gained, and for the lessons learned on how to crowdsource AI assistance in mathematics in a responsible fashion.

秉承我近期关于“数学 2.0”的演讲精神，也就是探索利用 AI 辅助来增进而非替代人类数学理解的新方式，我想通过这篇博文提出一项实验性挑战。它的灵感来自近期围绕排除朗道–西格尔零点的研究活动，相关讨论见此前这篇博文。

朗道–西格尔零点与第十判别式问题之间存在一种广为人知的联系，我在这里作简要回顾；有关这个问题的深入讨论，可以参阅 Shafarevich 的这篇综述。早在高斯的工作中，人们就注意到，有九个判别式 −d{-d}−d，其对应二次域 Q(−d){{\bf Q}(\sqrt{-d})}Q(−d​) 的整数环具有唯一分解性质，即

d=1,2,3,7,11,19,43,67,163.\displaystyle d = 1, 2, 3, 7, 11, 19, 43, 67, 163.d=1,2,3,7,11,19,43,67,163.

高斯相信，这九个数就是此类判别式的完整列表（即“类数一问题”），但直到 1952 年，Heegner 通过引入模曲线上的 Heegner 点这一概念，才证明了这一点；上述数如今称为 Heegner 数。

在 Heegner 的解答之前，Heilbronn 和 Linfoot 于 1934 年的工作利用了如今所谓的 Deuring–Heilbronn 排斥现象，证明如果上述列表中存在第十个判别式，就可以用它来“排斥”任何更多的此类判别式，因此在已知的九个 Heegner 数之外，至多还存在一个。然而，这个论证是非有效的（ineffective）：它没有给出这个假定存在的第十判别式的上界，因此仅凭这一结果，没有显而易见的方法来解决类数一问题。

这与朗道–西格尔零点有着密切的联系：如果 d{d}d 是一个 Heegner 数，而 χ{\chi}χ 是与该二次域对应的二次特征，那么类数公式给出 L(1,χ)=πd{L(1,\chi) = \frac{\pi}{\sqrt{d}}}L(1,χ)=d​π​，再稍作推导，就能得到一个朗道–西格尔零点 L(β,χ)=0{L(\beta,\chi)=0}L(β,χ)=0，满足 1−β≍1d{1-\beta \asymp \frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}}1−β≍d​1​。当 d{d}d 足够大时，这与西格尔定理矛盾；但该定理的标准证明也依赖于排斥现象，同样是非有效的。直到 1976 年，Goldfeld 才给出西格尔定理的一个有效版本：在给定一条合适的高秩椭圆曲线时，它至少足以对第十判别式给出一个界。在 Oesterlé 的后续工作中，结合 Gross 和 Zagier 的一条高秩椭圆曲线，得到了界 d≤e7000{d \leq e^{7000}}d≤e7000；使用后来 Brumer 和 Kramer 的一条椭圆曲线，其高秩性质由 Mestre、Oesterlé 和 Serre 证明，这个界被改进为 d≤e55{d \leq e^{55}}d≤e55。更多讨论见 Goldfeld 的这篇综述。

但现在，我们已经有相当多种新的（而且有效的）方法可以排除西格尔零点：或者建立准黎曼假设，或者使用此前这篇博文中论证的某个版本。原则上，这些论证的每个版本都应当也能给出第十判别式 d{d}d 的一个界（不使用 Heegner 对类数一问题的解答，因为该解答已经排除了这个判别式）。把每项估计都变得足够显式，从而得到这样的界，对人类而言是相当繁琐的任务。然而，它似乎非常适合 AI。

因此，我提出的挑战是：对于每一篇与准黎曼假设或朗道–西格尔零点问题有关的新手稿或消化整理稿（也可以采用某种全新的论证），使用 AI 工具，在不使用 Heegner 方法的情况下，为假设存在的第十判别式 d{d}d 推导一个有效上界，并尽最大努力优化这个常数。这有几个目的：

  • 如果这个阈值足够低，能通过直接计算处理剩余的判别式，那么这就会给出类数一问题的一个新解答；它或许还能以高质量的方式在 Lean 中形式化，例如达到适合贡献给 Mathlib 的程度。
  • 对每份不同的论述都开展这一练习，就能获得一种比较各份论述“强度”的方式（在分别完成各自可行的优化之后），这或许有助于了解不同方法编排方式的相对优劣。
  • 尽量减小上界的练习，可能促使人们创造全新的论证，用来解决类数一问题，或排除朗道–西格尔零点。
  • 此前曾有许多排除朗道–西格尔零点的“失败”尝试。其中有些可能包含能够与这一问题的新解法结合的思想，从而“修复”证明，或者至少改进第十判别式的界。
  • 这里的努力还可能有助于推进关于更高类数问题的认识。凭借 Watkins 在 2004 年的工作，目前类数不超过 100 的问题已经得到解决。

此外，由于这两个问题都已经解决（类数一问题甚至已解决七十多年），这样的竞赛意外干扰正在进行中的研究项目的风险很小。

因此，我邀请感兴趣的读者在评论中贡献：他们从现有论证或全新论证中能够提取出的、关于第十判别式的任何有效界。本次挑战允许任意程度的 AI 使用，只要加以披露。请注意，这项挑战不只是要在所有可能的方法中获得总体上“最佳”的界；我也关心从任何一个特定方法中能够提取出怎样的界，即使它比 Goldfeld 或 Oesterlé 的界更差。特别是，如果某个方法最终得到的界相当大，这样的“负面”结果仍然是具有科学价值、值得报告的信息。也可以探索其他方向，例如不使用大量计算来榨取最大程度的优化，而是追求足够简洁、使人类能够核验其论证的界。

任何大体量的输出都应以链接而不是纯文本的形式发布。例如，提供一个包含自己工作的 GitHub 仓库链接，会是一种合理的提交方式（但应在评论正文中附上一份由人撰写的摘要）。

我希望贡献者不要把速度置于质量之上；这并不是一场争夺谁“第一个”声称获得某个界的比赛。其精神应当是协作，而不是竞争：可以把它看作一个为 AI 时代更新的 Polymath 式项目。特别是，与传统数学研究一样，在先前贡献的基础上进一步工作，为某种证明技术得到更好的界，是恰当的。（当然，在这种情况下，应当对先前来源作适当的归功说明。）

我不会坚持要求对结果进行形式验证（例如用 Lean），不过，对于提交纯 AI 生成解答的人，我希望他们在将结果发布到这里之前，做好足够的尽职检查以核验输出，例如使用一个独立的 AI 工具。

我期待这项实验性挑战的结果，既期待它带来的数学洞见，也期待我们从中学到如何以负责任的方式，众包开展数学中的 AI 辅助工作。
