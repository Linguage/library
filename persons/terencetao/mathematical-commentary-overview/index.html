数学评论：数学的未来？

Linguista · 本站栏目导言

The introductory guide on this page was written for this site by Linguista. This collection includes mathematical commentary, guest articles, and public statements by Terence Tao and other authors, with authorship credited for each work. The reading-resource lists at the end identify their original author and sources separately.

What is the future of mathematics in the age of AI? The question concerns both what tools can accomplish and why people do mathematics: how we formulate worthwhile problems, develop understanding, educate the next generation, and judge the value of a contribution.

This collection brings together different authors’ reflections on these questions. Some begin with research practice and tools; others focus on education, careers, and academic communities; still others examine the meaning and value of mathematics. The authors offer their own judgments about technological prospects, risks, and possible responses. Reading these voices together can reveal different dimensions of the questions while leaving room for unresolved disagreements.

Read by theme

The collection currently contains 40 commentaries, guest articles, and public statements. The six groups below organize them by their main concern, with each article listed once and accompanied by a short reading note. Grouping does not imply agreement among authors, and many pieces span several themes. Tao’s three external resource lists remain at the end.

The purposes and values of mathematics

These essays examine proof, understanding, beauty, and personal motivation, asking whether mathematical value can be reduced to results or competition with machines.

  • After Math · Silvia De Toffoli and Eamon Duede De Toffoli and Duede distinguish logical certification from intelligible proof and reject reducing mathematics to a problem-solving game that AI can win.

  • Egoless Mathematics in the AI Era · Karthik C. S., Amit Sahai and Jie Xue Karthik, Sahai, and Xue explore an egoless approach grounded in mathematical truth and the joy of understanding rather than personal achievement or competitive superiority.

  • Happy, those able to know the causes of things · Nestor Guillen Guillen views language models as cultural technologies drawing on mathematical heritage, using a “convex hull of ideas” and creative-community history to examine innovation and the field’s future.

  • How AI does, and does not, change the way I do math · Rachel Webb Webb describes research as creating interesting mathematical narratives and explores how AI can change the process without displacing enjoyment, taste, or community.

  • A value-based approach to mathematics · Ivan Corwin Corwin examines mathematical value at societal, student, community, and individual levels, proposing that these values guide AI use, education, and resource allocation.

  • Why are we even doing all this? · Rikab Gambhir Gambhir satirizes the reduction of research to mass-produced facts and papers, urging readers to reconsider curiosity, creativity, beauty, and understanding.

  • Why I do mathematical research · Emily Riehl Riehl draws on category theory and theory-building to explain her pursuit of apt abstractions and simple explanations, and the value of helping others reach the frontier.

  • On the Hodge conjecture · Burt Totaro Burt Totaro reviews the theories and recent progress inspired by the Hodge conjecture and considers the value of difficult problems and commercial pressure around AI results.

  • Wimbledon, the U.S. Open, and the future of mathematics · Steven Strogatz Steven Strogatz uses Wimbledon and the U.S. Open to reflect on mathematical tradition, the openness AI may bring, and what might be lost amid change.

Research practices and new directions

These articles explore broader research ambitions, perceived barriers, and concrete experiments in AI-assisted mathematical work.

  • An experimental AI-math challenge: using tenth discriminant bounds to explore arguments ruling out Siegel zeroes · Terence Tao Tao proposes a collaborative AI-assisted challenge using effective bounds on the tenth discriminant to compare arguments about Siegel zeroes, with disclosure and verification requirements.

  • The barriers of perception · Raghu Meka Meka draws on personal research experiences to examine barriers created by reputation, convention, and failure, advocating renewed exploration through curiosity and new tools.

  • Fast math/slow math · Ben Antieau Antieau uses fast and slow food as a metaphor for combining ambitious AI-assisted research with sustained understanding, proposing standards for disclosure, authorship, and pace.

  • The Future of Mathematics · Jeremy Avigad Avigad places AI within the history of mathematical change and proposes harder problems, bigger ideas, and new practices as directions for the field.

Education and the next generation

These pieces address student motivation and careers, doctoral training, teaching conditions, and support for early-career researchers.

  • AI, Mathematics, Education, and a public goods problem · Gireeja Ranade Ranade frames the cultivation of broad and diverse human expertise as a public good requiring rigorous teaching, career support, and sustained investment.

  • Cognitive Sanctuaries or: Manifesto of the Department Chair · Jess Werk Werk draws on astronomy and departmental leadership to propose doctoral assessments, faculty incentives, and community practices that protect sustained thought and hands-on learning.

  • Mathematics is a subject for the young · Tristan Buckmaster Buckmaster places the next generation at the center of mathematics’ future, connecting new mathematical-physics opportunities with data protection, tool access, and fair recognition.

  • What does the advent of powerful AI models mean for mathematicians like me? · Jennifer Taback Taback draws on liberal-arts college experience to discuss AI’s research benefits, assessment difficulties, and the need to include diverse institutions and junior faculty in reform.

  • What should we tell our students? · Álvaro Lozano-Robledo Lozano-Robledo addresses five student concerns about AI, jobs, and doctoral study, emphasizing curiosity, understanding, and active research while acknowledging uncertainty.

Recognition, exposition, and communication

These essays translate the value of understanding into proposals for academic assessment, exposition, illustration, and public communication.

  • “Deep theorems were scarce and difficult and so became an effective mechanism to identify deep thought. AI has broken this system.” · Bryna Kra Kra argues that theorem production is becoming a weaker proxy for deep understanding and calls for explicit recognition of verification, exposition, and intellectual responsibility.

  • Illustration beyond Proof · Kate Stange and Edmund Harriss Stange and Harriss use mathematical illustration, making, and critique to examine research and learning beyond proof, including rigor and academic recognition.

  • Let the Diners Into the Kitchen · Noah Giansiracusa Giansiracusa uses a restaurant-kitchen metaphor to advocate blogs, media engagement, and research backstories that reveal mathematics as a collaborative human activity.

  • If math is more than proof, we need to better celebrate the rest of it · Grant Sanderson Sanderson advocates greater academic recognition for motivated explanations, proposing expository problems and reforms in teaching, publishing, and hiring.

  • Mathematics has changed. Let’s act like it · Frank Calegari Calegari calls for reforms in hiring, journals, graduate education, and seminars, assessing contributions through breadth and structural understanding in an AI-transformed field.

Community norms and public infrastructure

These articles debate release practices, credit, privacy, and open collaboration, and propose public AI resources and mathematical repositories.

  • A CERN for AI-assisted science? · Dimitris Koukoulopoulos Koukoulopoulos proposes publicly funded frontier AI and computing infrastructure to improve access, confidentiality, and scientific independence.

  • Becoming a benchmark · Talia Ringer Ringer draws on programming-language research becoming an AI benchmark to discuss protecting disciplinary culture through public communication and participatory tool design.

  • Disadvantages of Designating Mathematicians for AI Output Releases · Advisory Group on Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence The Advisory Group on Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence critiques company-designated mathematicians in AI releases and recommends public archiving and independently organized follow-up work.

  • Changes to the Erdős problems web site · Thomas Bloom Bloom explains changes to comments, proof claims, status labels, and exposition intended to refocus the Erdős problems website on human discussion and understanding.

  • On the existence of non-sofic groups · Andreas Thom Thom recounts disputes over credit and questions about data transparency surrounding the non-sofic groups release, then reflects on authorship, assessment, and public AI governance.

  • Hexagon · Ben Antieau Antieau introduces Hexagon’s archiving, submission, and contributor policies as a community-run home for AI-assisted mathematical results awaiting fuller human understanding.

  • Open problems, open mathematics · Antonio Auffinger Auffinger draws on technological change and openness norms in biology to explore protecting collaboration from priority pressures and finding new questions across disciplines.

  • A Severe Misalignment of AI in Mathematics · Terence Tao (introduction); 25 Fields Medallists (statement) The collective statement introduced by Tao criticizes misalignment between AI problem-solving benchmarks and mathematical goals, emphasizing understanding, credit, training, and responsible releases.

  • Why I didn’t sign the Fields medallists’ letter · Timothy Gowers Gowers explains his decision not to sign the collective letter, defends diverse problem-solving and understanding motivations, and highlights risks to training and institutional support.

  • Why I agreed to join AGMAI · Martin Hairer Martin Hairer explains AGMAI’s independence, advisory role, and limitations, and why he chose to engage in dialogue between mathematicians and AI labs.

  • This is normal bad work · Sophie Huiberts Sophie Huiberts critiques OpenAI’s releases, attribution, and exposition through the responsibilities of mathematical authors toward the literature.

Reliability, social responsibility, and human agency

These pieces examine the limits of formal verification and the need for mathematical communities able to scrutinize, explain, and guide technology.

  • Will it be a golden age or a dark age for mathematics (and science)? · Francesco Maggi Maggi contrasts golden-age and dark-age scenarios to examine how mathematization, human understanding, training, and public infrastructure could shape science.

  • Headlines and inside stories: understanding and trust in AI for mathematics, science, and engineering · Tapio Schneider Schneider compares proofs, weather forecasts, climate models, and engineering design to explain when testable predictions suffice and when auditable causal understanding is also needed.

  • What mathematicians should know about the Lean Theorem Prover: questions of reliability and AI · Thomas Hales Hales explains Lean’s formalization, kernel, and type-theoretic foundations, examining why statement audits, cross-checking, and kernel verification still require human understanding and oversight.

  • We’re gonna need a lot more mathematicians · Amit Sahai Sahai uses consequential technological decisions to argue for more mathematicians capable of collectively understanding unfamiliar ideas and sustaining independent judgment and human agency.

  • The technical debt of AI-generated mathematics · Henry Cohn Cohn uses technical debt to explain how opaque AI proofs shift the costs of understanding and scrutiny onto the community, arguing that contributors must help integrate their results into shared knowledge.

  • Why Do We Need Human Mathematicians Anymore? · Po-Shen Loh Loh starts from human flourishing to argue for active expert communities capable of overseeing technology and setting its direction, with implications for education and professional practice.

Suggested comparative reading

  • Understanding and problem-solving: Read Riehl and De Toffoli and Duede alongside Gowers’s response to compare accounts of the pleasure of solving problems and the value of understanding.
  • From principles to institutions: Follow Kra and Sanderson on assessment with Calegari’s reform proposals, then Werk and Ranade on the conditions needed to educate future researchers.
  • Using tools responsibly: Compare Hales on formal verification, Cohn on technical debt, and Tao’s experimental challenge to consider how scrutiny, explanation, and collaboration can work together.
  • Who shapes mathematics’ future: The collective statement, Gowers’s dissent, AGMAI’s release recommendations, and proposals for public infrastructure reveal different judgments and courses of action within the community.

Crowdsourcing a list of general resources on the purpose, value, and nature of mathematics

Terence Tao · September 12, 2026 · Original post

Parallel to the other crowdsourced resource drive on this blog, I would like to collect books, writings and other resources that discuss or explain the purpose(s), value(s), and nature of mathematics. As seen in reactions to recent events, one can see many oversimplified or inaccurate perceptions of mathematics, such as

  • “Mathematical research is about solving open problems.”
  • “The main reason for posing an open problem is to obtain its solution.”
  • “We should only work on problems that are of immediate benefit to society.”
  • “We should prioritize maximizing the number of problems solved.”

The question of what mathematics actually is is far more complex than these assertions might suggest. Here are some classic books and articles on these topics:

  • Vladimir Arnold, “On teaching mathematics”, 1997 (somewhat provocative in tone, but still interesting)
  • Philip Davis, Reuben Hersh, “The Mathematical Experience”, 1981
  • Timothy Gowers, “The two cultures of mathematics”, 2000
  • Timothy Gowers, “Mathematics: a very short introduction”, 2002
  • G. H. Hardy, “A Mathematician’s Apology”, 1940 (somewhat dated at this point, but still interesting)
  • Reuben Hersh, “What is mathematics, really?”, 1997
  • David Hilbert, “Mathematical Problems”, 1900. (Most famous for the list of 23 problems themselves, but the preamble is highly recommended reading. For a deeper dive into the quote “A mathematical theory is not to be considered complete until you have made it so clear that you can explain it to the first man whom you meet on the street.” that Hilbert attributed to “an old French mathematician”, see this article of Barrow-Green and Siegmund-Scholze.)
  • Imre Lakatos, “Proofs and refutations”, 1976
  • Bill Thurston, “On proof and progress in mathematics”, 1994 (see also his response to the MathOverflow question “What’s a mathematician to do?”)
  • Cedric Villani, “Birth of a Theorem”, 2015

Here are some more informal links:

  • Matt Might, The illustrated guide to a Ph.D., 2010.
  • A collaboration between myself and Zach Wienersmith on sphere packing, as a five part comic strip (Apr 9, 2026)
  • Illustrating the impact of the mathematical sciences – a series of posters by the National Academy of Sciences. (I was on the committee to create these posters.) (2023)

I can also list a few writings, posts, and interviews I have done on these topics:

  • There is more to mathematics than rigour and proofs (2007)
  • What is good mathematics? (2007)
  • The value in “Dumb” questions (2022)
  • What makes for ‘Good’ Mathematics (2024)
  • What does it mean to think like a mathematician? (Mar 14, 2026)
  • Interview with “Big Think” (relating to my forthcoming book) (Aug 28, 2026) – see also this abridged version
  • On remembering what it is like to be a child: curiosity-driven play, the “largest number” game, and what premature optimization for the nominal goal extinguishes (Sep 9, 2026)

AI 时代，数学的未来会是什么样？这个问题既关乎工具能够完成什么，也关乎人为什么做数学：怎样提出值得研究的问题，怎样形成理解，怎样培养下一代，以及怎样判断一项工作的价值。

本栏目汇集不同作者围绕这些问题展开的思考。有些文章从研究实践和工具出发，有些关注教育、职业与学术共同体，还有一些追问数学的意义与价值。作者对技术前景、风险和应对方式各有判断。把这些声音放在一起，能够帮助读者看见问题的不同层面，也保留其中尚未解决的分歧。

本栏目现收录 40 篇评论、客座文章与公开声明。下面按各篇的主要关注点分为六组，每篇列入一个主类，并附简短导读；分类不代表作者立场相同，也不排除文章横跨多个主题。文末另保留陶哲轩整理的三份外部资源清单。

数学的目的与价值

从证明、理解、审美与个人动机出发，追问数学的价值是否能用解题成果或人与机器的竞赛来衡量。

  • 数学之后（After Math） · Silvia De Toffoli and Eamon Duede De Toffoli 与 Duede 区分逻辑认证与可理解的证明，反对把数学简化为可被 AI 赢下的解题游戏。

  • AI 时代的无我数学 · Karthik C. S., Amit Sahai and Jie Xue Karthik、Sahai 与 Xue 从数学真理的独立性出发，探讨超越个人功绩与竞争、以求真和理解之乐为中心的无我数学。

  • 能知晓事物原因的人是幸福的 · Nestor Guillen Guillen 将大语言模型理解为汇集数学遗产的文化技术，以“思想的凸包”和创造性社群的历史讨论创新与共同体的未来。

  • AI 如何改变、又如何不改变我做数学的方式 · Rachel Webb Webb 将研究描述为创造有趣的数学叙事，讨论 AI 如何改变工作过程而不必改变个人乐趣、数学品味与社群联系。

  • 以价值为导向的数学之路 · Ivan Corwin Corwin 从社会、学生、共同体和个人四个层面梳理数学的价值，主张据此引导 AI 使用、教育与资源分配。

  • 我们做这一切究竟是为了什么？ · Rikab Gambhir Gambhir 以讽刺笔法批评把科研等同于批量生成事实与论文，提醒读者重新思考好奇、创造、美感与理解的意义。

  • 我为什么做数学研究 · Emily Riehl Riehl 以范畴论和理论建设为例，阐述寻找恰当抽象与简单解释的个人追求，以及帮助他人抵达数学前沿的价值。

  • 论霍奇猜想 · Burt Totaro Burt Totaro 回顾霍奇猜想所激发的理论与近期进展，讨论探索难题的价值及 AI 成果发布的商业压力。

  • 温布尔登、美网与数学的未来 · Steven Strogatz Steven Strogatz 以温布尔登和美网为喻，思考数学传统、AI 带来的开放机会，以及变化中的珍贵事物。

研究方式与新方向

讨论如何借助 AI 扩大研究视野、突破认知障碍，并以具体合作实验探索新的数学实践。

  • 一项实验性 AI 数学挑战：用第十判别式的界探索排除西格尔零点的论证 · Terence Tao 陶哲轩提出以第十判别式有效上界比较和理解排除西格尔零点的论证，并强调协作、披露与结果核验的实验规则。

  • 认知的障碍 · Raghu Meka Meka 结合个人研究经历，反思名望、惯例与失败如何制造认知障碍，并主张以好奇心和新工具重新探索可能性。

  • 快数学，慢数学 · Ben Antieau Antieau 以快食与慢食为比喻，讨论宏大 AI 辅助研究和缓慢理解如何共存，并提出披露、署名和研究节奏的共同标准。

  • 数学的未来 · Jeremy Avigad Avigad 将 AI 放入数学技术变迁的历史，提出解决更难问题、思考更宏大思想和尝试新实践的未来方向。

教育与下一代

聚焦学生的学习与职业焦虑、博士培养、教学条件，以及保护青年研究者成长所需的支持。

  • AI、数学、教育与公共品问题 · Gireeja Ranade Ranade 将培养广泛而多样的人类专业能力视为公共品，呼吁以扎实教学、就业支持与持续投入维系学生的学习动力。

  • 认知庇护所，或：系主任宣言 · Jess Werk Werk 借鉴天文学与院系管理经验，提出改革博士考核、教师奖励和社群活动，为缓慢思考与亲身实践提供制度保护。

  • 数学是一门属于年轻人的学科 · Tristan Buckmaster Buckmaster 将数学的未来寄托于下一代，讨论数学与物理的新机会，以及数据保护、工具获取和贡献认可对青年研究者的重要性。

  • 强大 AI 模型的出现，对像我这样的数学家意味着什么？ · Jennifer Taback Taback 从文理学院教师的日常经验出发，讨论 AI 的研究帮助、教学考核难题和不同院校及青年教师应被纳入的制度讨论。

  • 我们应该对学生说些什么？ · Álvaro Lozano-Robledo Lozano-Robledo 回应学生关于 AI、工作和博士道路的五类担忧，在承认预测不确定性的同时强调热爱、理解与主动参与研究。

评价、阐释与传播

把“重视理解”落实到学术评价、讲解、图示与公众沟通，讨论哪些贡献应得到认可。

  • “深刻的定理稀缺而难得，因此成了识别深刻思考的一种有效机制。AI 打破了这个体系。” · Bryna Kra Kra 认为定理产出与深刻理解的联系正在松动，主张在出版、招聘与奖励中更明确认可核验、阐释和思想责任。

  • 证明之外的图示 · Kate Stange and Edmund Harriss Stange 与 Harriss 以数学图示、制作和作品评议为例，说明证明之外的研究与学习方式及其严谨性和认可问题。

  • 让食客走进厨房 · Noah Giansiracusa Giansiracusa 用餐厅厨房的比喻，呼吁通过博客、媒体和研究幕后故事向公众展现数学的协作过程与人文价值。

  • 如果数学不只是证明，我们就需要更好地重视其余部分 · Grant Sanderson Sanderson 主张为交代思想来由的讲解赋予更高学术地位，并通过阐释问题、教学、期刊与招聘改革奖励理解的传播。

  • 数学已经改变，让我们据此行动 · Frank Calegari Calegari 呼吁把 AI 带来的变化落实为招聘、期刊、研究生培养与研讨班改革，并以广阔视野和结构性理解评价贡献。

共同体规范与公共基础设施

围绕成果发布、归功、隐私与开放合作展开争论，并介绍公共 AI 和数学成果平台的建设方案。

  • 为 AI 辅助科学建立一个 CERN？ · Dimitris Koukoulopoulos Koukoulopoulos 提议建立公共资助、由科学界治理的前沿 AI 与算力设施，以改善研究获取机会、保密保障和学术独立性。

  • 成为评测基准 · Talia Ringer Ringer 从编程语言研究成为 AI 评测对象的经历出发，讨论如何通过公众沟通和参与式工具设计维护学科文化与目标。

  • 指定数学家参与 AI 成果发布的弊端 · Advisory Group on Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence 数学与人工智能咨询小组分析由 AI 公司指定数学家参与成果发布的弊端，建议公开存档并由独立共同体组织后续理解工作。

  • Erdős 问题网站的调整 · Thomas Bloom Bloom 解释暂停问题评论和证明声明、取消状态标签并加强讲解的调整，以将 Erdős 问题网站重新聚焦于人类交流与理解。

  • 关于非 sofic 群的存在性 · Andreas Thom Thom 回顾非 sofic 群成果发布中的归功争议与数据透明度疑问，并据此反思作者身份、学术评价和 AI 公共治理。

  • Hexagon · Ben Antieau Antieau 介绍 Hexagon 的存档、投稿与贡献者机制，为尚待人类理解的 AI 辅助数学成果提供共同体维护的存储空间。

  • 开放的问题，开放的数学 · Antonio Auffinger Auffinger 借鉴生物学的技术变迁与开放规范，讨论如何避免抢先发表压力损害数学合作，并从跨学科交流中寻找新问题。

  • 数学领域 AI 的严重目标错位 · Terence Tao（引言）；25位菲尔兹奖得主（声明） 这份由陶哲轩介绍的联名声明批评 AI 解题评测与数学共同体目标的错位，强调理解、归功、人才培养和负责任的成果发布。

  • 为什么我没有签署菲尔兹奖得主的联名信 · Timothy Gowers Gowers 解释未签署联名信的理由，维护解题与理解两种动机的多样性，并将主要风险指向传承、学生动力与支持数学的社会结构。

  • 我为什么同意加入 AGMAI · Martin Hairer Martin Hairer 解释 AGMAI 的独立性、咨询角色与局限，并阐述参与数学共同体和 AI 实验室对话的理由。

  • 这不过是寻常的劣质工作 · Sophie Huiberts Sophie Huiberts 从数学作者的阐释与文献责任出发，批评 OpenAI 的成果发布、归功和呈现质量。

可靠性、社会责任与人的能动性

讨论形式验证与人类理解的边界，以及社会为何需要能够审查、解释和引导技术的数学共同体。

  • 数学（与科学）将迎来黄金时代，还是黑暗时代？ · Francesco Maggi Maggi 以黄金时代与黑暗时代两种图景，讨论数学化、人类理解、青年培养和公共基础设施将如何塑造科学的未来。

  • 标题与内情：数学、科学和工程领域对 AI 的理解与信任 · Tapio Schneider Schneider 比较数学证明、天气预报、气候模拟和工程设计，说明何时可检验的预测足够、何时还必须具备可审查的因果理解。

  • 数学家应当了解的 Lean 定理证明器：可靠性与 AI 问题 · Thomas Hales Hales 介绍 Lean 的形式化、内核和类型论基础，讨论命题审计、交叉检查及内核验证为何仍需要人类理解与监督。

  • 我们将需要多得多的数学家 · Amit Sahai Sahai 以重大技术决策为例，主张培养更多能共同理解陌生思想的数学家，以维护独立专业判断和人类能动性。

  • AI 生成数学的技术债务 · Henry Cohn Cohn 用技术债务解释难以理解的 AI 证明为何把消化与核验成本转嫁给共同体，并要求发布者为成果融入知识体系承担责任。

  • 我们为什么还需要人类数学家？ · Po-Shen Loh Loh 以促进人类蓬勃发展为原则，论证技术监督与方向选择需要持续活跃的人类专家共同体，并讨论相应教育和职业改变。

几条对照阅读路线

  • 理解与解题：可将 Riehl、De Toffoli 与 Duede 的文章和 Gowers 的回应放在一起，比较不同作者如何看待解题的乐趣与理解的价值。
  • 从理念到制度：从 Kra 与 Sanderson 对学术评价的讨论，读到 Calegari 的具体改革建议，再参照 Werk 与 Ranade 对培养条件的关注。
  • 如何负责任地使用工具：将 Hales 的形式验证分析、Cohn 的技术债务论述与陶哲轩的实验性挑战对照，思考核验、解释与合作如何结合。
  • 谁来决定数学的未来：联名声明、Gowers 的异议、AGMAI 的发布建议和公共基础设施提案，呈现了共同体内部不同的判断与行动方向。

众包征集关于数学的目的、价值与本质的一般资源清单

陶哲轩 · 2026 年 9 月 12 日 · 原文

与本博客上另一项众包资源征集活动并行，我希望收集一些讨论或阐释数学的目的、价值与本质的书籍、文章及其他资源。从人们对近期事件的反应中，可以看到许多对数学过度简化或不准确的认识，例如：

  • “数学研究就是解决开放问题。”
  • “提出开放问题的主要理由，就是得到它的解答。”
  • “我们只应该研究能够立即造福社会的问题。”
  • “我们应该优先考虑最大化所解决问题的数量。”

数学究竟是什么，这个问题远比上述断言所暗示的复杂。下面是一些讨论这些话题的经典书籍和文章：

  • Vladimir Arnold：《论数学教学》，1997 年（语气颇具挑衅性，但仍然很有意思）。
  • Philip Davis、Reuben Hersh：《数学经验》，1981 年。
  • Timothy Gowers：《数学的两种文化》，2000 年。
  • Timothy Gowers：《数学：非常简短的导论》，2002 年。
  • G. H. Hardy：《一个数学家的辩白》，1940 年（如今看来有些过时，但仍然很有意思）。
  • Reuben Hersh：《数学究竟是什么？》，1997 年。
  • David Hilbert：《数学问题》，1900 年。（最著名的当然是其中列出的 23 个问题，但我强烈推荐阅读其序言。希尔伯特曾引用一位“法国老数学家”的话：“一种数学理论，只有当你把它讲得足够清楚，能够向在街上遇到的第一个人解释时，才能算是完备的。”如果想更深入地了解这句话，请参阅 Barrow-Green 与 Siegmund-Scholze 的这篇文章。）
  • Imre Lakatos：《证明与反驳》，1976 年。
  • Bill Thurston：《论数学中的证明与进步》，1994 年（另请参阅他对 MathOverflow 问题“数学家该做些什么？”的回答）。
  • Cedric Villani：《一个定理的诞生》，2015 年。

下面是一些更为轻松的链接：

  • Matt Might：《博士学位图解指南》，2010 年。
  • 我与 Zach Wienersmith 合作创作的球堆积主题五篇连载漫画（2026 年 4 月 9 日）。
  • 《图说数学科学的影响》：美国国家科学院制作的一系列海报。（我是负责制作这些海报的委员会成员之一。）（2023 年）

我还可以列出自己就这些话题写过的一些文章、博文，以及接受过的一些访谈：

  • 《数学不仅仅是严谨与证明》（2007 年）。
  • 《什么是好的数学？》（2007 年）。
  • 《“傻”问题的价值》（2022 年）。
  • 《怎样才算“好的”数学》（2024 年）。
  • 《像数学家一样思考意味着什么？》（2026 年 3 月 14 日）。
  • 接受“Big Think”的访谈（与我即将出版的书有关）（2026 年 8 月 28 日）；另请参阅这个节选版。
  • 《记住孩童时的感受：好奇心驱动的游戏、“最大的数”游戏，以及过早针对名义目标进行优化会熄灭什么》（2026 年 9 月 9 日）。
