数学评论：数学的未来？

Linguista · 本站栏目导言

The introductory guide on this page was written for this site by Linguista. This collection includes mathematical commentary, guest articles, and public statements by Terence Tao and other authors, with authorship credited for each work. The reading-resource lists at the end identify their original author and sources separately.

What is the future of mathematics in the age of AI? The question concerns both what tools can accomplish and why people do mathematics: how we formulate worthwhile problems, develop understanding, educate the next generation, and judge the value of a contribution.

This collection brings together different authors’ reflections on these questions. Some begin with research practice and tools; others focus on education, careers, and academic communities; still others examine the meaning and value of mathematics. The authors offer their own judgments about technological prospects, risks, and possible responses. Reading these voices together can reveal different dimensions of the questions while leaving room for unresolved disagreements.

Research: What do the tools change?

When AI participates in finding ideas, attempting proofs, or organizing knowledge, how might researchers’ work change? Which steps can be entrusted to tools, and which judgments require closer scrutiny? How does the reliability offered by formal verification relate to judgments about research questions, methods, and results?

  • Rachel Webb: How AI does, and does not, change the way I do math
  • Thomas Hales: What mathematicians should know about the Lean Theorem Prover: questions of reliability and AI

Understanding: What comes after a proof?

How are a correct conclusion, a checkable proof, and mathematical understanding connected? What roles do explanations, illustrations, concepts, and intuition play? A discussion of mathematics’ future can begin with these familiar and fundamental questions.

  • Jeremy Avigad: The Future of Mathematics
  • Kate Stange and Edmund Harriss: Illustration beyond Proof

Education: What should the next generation learn?

If the tools used in learning and research change, how should classrooms, exercises, and research training respond? How can students develop independent judgment while drawing on those tools? For people choosing research directions and careers, which expectations and uncertainties need an honest discussion?

  • Álvaro Lozano-Robledo: What should we tell our students?
  • Jennifer Taback: What does the advent of powerful AI models mean for mathematicians like me?

Evaluation: Which contributions deserve recognition?

When the ways of producing results change, should the ways of assessing contributions change too? Alongside proving theorems, what recognition should be given to posing questions, explaining ideas, building tools, and fostering communication? Such choices influence where researchers invest their time.

  • Grant Sanderson: If math is more than proof, we need to better celebrate the rest of it
  • Ivan Corwin: A value-based approach to mathematics

Institutions: How does the mathematical community choose?

The future of mathematics also depends on decisions made by universities, research platforms, and academic communities. How should they support learning and exploration, organize open problems and results, and allocate attention and resources? These institutional questions affect who can participate in mathematics and what kinds of work can be sustained.

  • Jess Werk: Cognitive Sanctuaries or: Manifesto of the Department Chair
  • Thomas Bloom: Changes to the Erdős problems web site

These themes overlap. Readers can start with their own concerns or compare different authors’ responses to the same question. The question mark in this introduction’s title leaves space for a discussion that is still unfolding.

Crowdsourcing a list of general resources on the purpose, value, and nature of mathematics

Terence Tao · September 12, 2026 · Original post

Parallel to the other crowdsourced resource drive on this blog, I would like to collect books, writings and other resources that discuss or explain the purpose(s), value(s), and nature of mathematics. As seen in reactions to recent events, one can see many oversimplified or inaccurate perceptions of mathematics, such as

  • “Mathematical research is about solving open problems.”
  • “The main reason for posing an open problem is to obtain its solution.”
  • “We should only work on problems that are of immediate benefit to society.”
  • “We should prioritize maximizing the number of problems solved.”

The question of what mathematics actually is is far more complex than these assertions might suggest. Here are some classic books and articles on these topics:

  • Vladimir Arnold, “On teaching mathematics”, 1997 (somewhat provocative in tone, but still interesting)
  • Philip Davis, Reuben Hersh, “The Mathematical Experience”, 1981
  • Timothy Gowers, “The two cultures of mathematics”, 2000
  • Timothy Gowers, “Mathematics: a very short introduction”, 2002
  • G. H. Hardy, “A Mathematician’s Apology”, 1940 (somewhat dated at this point, but still interesting)
  • Reuben Hersh, “What is mathematics, really?”, 1997
  • David Hilbert, “Mathematical Problems”, 1900. (Most famous for the list of 23 problems themselves, but the preamble is highly recommended reading. For a deeper dive into the quote “A mathematical theory is not to be considered complete until you have made it so clear that you can explain it to the first man whom you meet on the street.” that Hilbert attributed to “an old French mathematician”, see this article of Barrow-Green and Siegmund-Scholze.)
  • Imre Lakatos, “Proofs and refutations”, 1976
  • Bill Thurston, “On proof and progress in mathematics”, 1994 (see also his response to the MathOverflow question “What’s a mathematician to do?”)
  • Cedric Villani, “Birth of a Theorem”, 2015

Here are some more informal links:

  • Matt Might, The illustrated guide to a Ph.D., 2010.
  • A collaboration between myself and Zach Wienersmith on sphere packing, as a five part comic strip (Apr 9, 2026)
  • Illustrating the impact of the mathematical sciences – a series of posters by the National Academy of Sciences. (I was on the committee to create these posters.) (2023)

I can also list a few writings, posts, and interviews I have done on these topics:

  • There is more to mathematics than rigour and proofs (2007)
  • What is good mathematics? (2007)
  • The value in “Dumb” questions (2022)
  • What makes for ‘Good’ Mathematics (2024)
  • What does it mean to think like a mathematician? (Mar 14, 2026)
  • Interview with “Big Think” (relating to my forthcoming book) (Aug 28, 2026) – see also this abridged version
  • On remembering what it is like to be a child: curiosity-driven play, the “largest number” game, and what premature optimization for the nominal goal extinguishes (Sep 9, 2026)

Please contribute more links in the comments below!

Additional recent pieces on math and AI

  • Harald Helfgott, “Mathematics and the AI crisis”
  • Gil Kalai, ““Be a Grothendieck!”—On AI and Mathematics”

本篇的栏目导言由 Linguista 编写。栏目收录陶哲轩及其他作者的数学评论、客座文章和公开声明，均保留各篇实际署名；文末的阅读资源索引另行标明原作者与来源。

AI 时代，数学的未来会是什么样？这个问题既关乎工具能够完成什么，也关乎人为什么做数学：怎样提出值得研究的问题，怎样形成理解，怎样培养下一代，以及怎样判断一项工作的价值。

本栏目汇集不同作者围绕这些问题展开的思考。有些文章从研究实践和工具出发，有些关注教育、职业与学术共同体，还有一些追问数学的意义与价值。作者对技术前景、风险和应对方式各有判断。把这些声音放在一起，能够帮助读者看见问题的不同层面，也保留其中尚未解决的分歧。

研究：工具改变了什么？

当 AI 参与寻找思路、尝试证明或整理知识，研究者的工作会如何变化？哪些步骤可以交给工具，哪些判断仍需要仔细审视？形式化验证提供的可靠性，与研究中对问题、方法和结果的判断，又是什么关系？

  • Rachel Webb：AI 如何改变、又如何不改变我做数学的方式
  • Thomas Hales：数学家应当了解的 Lean 定理证明器：可靠性与 AI 问题

理解：得到证明之后，还需要什么？

一个正确的结论、一份可检验的证明和对数学的理解，彼此如何联系？解释、图示、概念与直觉在其中扮演什么角色？讨论数学的未来，也可以从这些日常而根本的问题开始。

  • Jeremy Avigad：数学的未来
  • Kate Stange 与 Edmund Harriss：证明之外的图示

教育：下一代应该学会什么？

如果学习和研究所使用的工具发生变化，课堂、练习与研究训练应当怎样回应？学生如何在借助工具的同时发展独立判断？对于正在选择研究方向与职业道路的人，又有哪些期待和不确定性需要坦诚讨论？

  • Álvaro Lozano-Robledo：我们应该对学生说些什么？
  • Jennifer Taback：强大 AI 模型的出现，对像我这样的数学家意味着什么？

评价：什么样的贡献值得重视？

当生产结果的方式改变，我们用来衡量贡献的尺度是否也应调整？除了证明定理，提出问题、说明思想、构建工具与促进交流，应当获得怎样的认可？这些选择会影响研究者投入时间的方向。

  • Grant Sanderson：如果数学不只是证明，我们就需要更好地重视其余部分
  • Ivan Corwin：以价值为导向的数学之路

制度：数学共同体如何作出选择？

数学的未来也取决于大学、研究平台与学术共同体的决定。怎样支持学习和探索，怎样组织开放的问题与成果，怎样分配注意力与资源？这些制度问题，会影响谁能参与数学，以及什么样的工作能够持续开展。

  • Jess Werk：认知庇护所，或：系主任宣言
  • Thomas Bloom：Erdős 问题网站的调整

这些主题彼此交织。读者可以从自己的关注点出发，也可以把不同作者对同一个问题的回答放在一起阅读。标题中的问号，为这场仍在展开的讨论保留了空间。

众包征集关于数学的目的、价值与本质的一般资源清单

陶哲轩 · 2026 年 9 月 12 日 · 原文

与本博客上另一项众包资源征集活动并行，我希望收集一些讨论或阐释数学的目的、价值与本质的书籍、文章及其他资源。从人们对近期事件的反应中，可以看到许多对数学过度简化或不准确的认识，例如：

  • “数学研究就是解决开放问题。”
  • “提出开放问题的主要理由，就是得到它的解答。”
  • “我们只应该研究能够立即造福社会的问题。”
  • “我们应该优先考虑最大化所解决问题的数量。”

数学究竟是什么，这个问题远比上述断言所暗示的复杂。下面是一些讨论这些话题的经典书籍和文章：

  • Vladimir Arnold：《论数学教学》，1997 年（语气颇具挑衅性，但仍然很有意思）。
  • Philip Davis、Reuben Hersh：《数学经验》，1981 年。
  • Timothy Gowers：《数学的两种文化》，2000 年。
  • Timothy Gowers：《数学：非常简短的导论》，2002 年。
  • G. H. Hardy：《一个数学家的辩白》，1940 年（如今看来有些过时，但仍然很有意思）。
  • Reuben Hersh：《数学究竟是什么？》，1997 年。
  • David Hilbert：《数学问题》，1900 年。（最著名的当然是其中列出的 23 个问题，但我强烈推荐阅读其序言。希尔伯特曾引用一位“法国老数学家”的话：“一种数学理论，只有当你把它讲得足够清楚，能够向在街上遇到的第一个人解释时，才能算是完备的。”如果想更深入地了解这句话，请参阅 Barrow-Green 与 Siegmund-Scholze 的这篇文章。）
  • Imre Lakatos：《证明与反驳》，1976 年。
  • Bill Thurston：《论数学中的证明与进步》，1994 年（另请参阅他对 MathOverflow 问题“数学家该做些什么？”的回答）。
  • Cedric Villani：《一个定理的诞生》，2015 年。

下面是一些更为轻松的链接：

  • Matt Might：《博士学位图解指南》，2010 年。
  • 我与 Zach Wienersmith 合作创作的球堆积主题五篇连载漫画（2026 年 4 月 9 日）。
  • 《图说数学科学的影响》：美国国家科学院制作的一系列海报。（我是负责制作这些海报的委员会成员之一。）（2023 年）

我还可以列出自己就这些话题写过的一些文章、博文，以及接受过的一些访谈：

  • 《数学不仅仅是严谨与证明》（2007 年）。
  • 《什么是好的数学？》（2007 年）。
  • 《“傻”问题的价值》（2022 年）。
  • 《怎样才算“好的”数学》（2024 年）。
  • 《像数学家一样思考意味着什么？》（2026 年 3 月 14 日）。
  • 接受“Big Think”的访谈（与我即将出版的书有关）（2026 年 8 月 28 日）；另请参阅这个节选版。
  • 《记住孩童时的感受：好奇心驱动的游戏、“最大的数”游戏，以及过早针对名义目标进行优化会熄灭什么》（2026 年 9 月 9 日）。
